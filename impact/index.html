Research deep dive / UCD Innovation Academy

Soiléir Impact Rating: making sustainability impact understandable.

Soiléir was explored in the context of my MSc Design Thinking for Sustainability and a Dublin City Council-related project brief. The central challenge was how to make sustainability performance and impact easier to interpret, compare and discuss without reducing complex environmental and social outcomes to a misleading single number.

Impact ratings can help people make decisions, but they also introduce design risks: what gets measured, which perspectives count, how evidence is weighted and whether a score creates false confidence. The research direction therefore centred on clarity, transparency and the relationship between evidence, interpretation and action.

Context, contribution and learning

Situation and challenge. Sustainability decisions involve trade-offs between environmental, social and practical outcomes. The design problem was how to make those trade-offs legible for different audiences, while recognising that evidence quality and context matter.

My involvement. The work sits within my postgraduate sustainability design practice, drawing on systems thinking, design research, framing and communication of complex information. The public record here does not establish a final approved rating methodology or implementation.

Approach. The relevant design activities include interrogating the decision context, identifying stakeholder information needs, mapping the relationships between impact dimensions and exploring ways to communicate uncertainty rather than conceal it.

Outcome and learning. The key portfolio learning is that impact assessment should help people ask better questions, not merely display a score. A credible future version would need documented stakeholder validation, evidence standards, transparent weighting and testing of comprehension across audiences.

Community research / X-hunt

Connecting willingness to contribute with real opportunity.

X-hunt began with a practical observation: people can have useful skills and a genuine desire to contribute yet still struggle to find the right opportunities. Community organisations, meanwhile, face difficulties making needs visible, coordinating contributors and recognising participation.

My research has explored both sides of this relationship through stakeholder conversations, surveys, empathy mapping and iterative concept development. The product direction is a privacy-conscious way for individuals to discover meaningful opportunities and for organisations to coordinate community contributions.

The learning is that participation depends on more than matching skills. Trust, motivation, time, accessibility, belonging and recognition all shape whether someone takes part and returns.

Mentorship and community engagement complement my commercial work. Supporting emerging designers creates opportunities to discuss research practice, career development, design judgement and the importance of accessibility and ethical decision-making.

01 / The context

Scientific knowledge doesn't arrive ready to use.

Scientific findings need interpretation before they become practical learning materials. In the existing process, professional experts reviewed scientific sources and manually condensed them into presentations and other resources for frontline workers.

That work matters particularly in HIV research, where the evidence can evolve while patients are receiving treatment. Making an update understandable and available at the right time is a real experience challenge—but it does not mean that an interface can replace professional expertise.

02 / What I noticed

The bottleneck wasn't only a document.

My interest was in the whole path from research to understanding. Experts were performing important interpretive work, but they also spent time turning that work into reusable communications. I wanted to explore whether AI could support the translation and production steps without confusing automated output with expert judgement.

The question shifted from 'how do we summarise research?' to 'how can reviewed knowledge be communicated in formats that work for the people receiving it?'

The opportunity wasn't to replace expertise. It was to make expert knowledge easier to understand and reuse.
03 / My contribution

Designing a direction for better knowledge translation.

I examined the manual process and explored an AI-assisted experience that could translate complex research into easy-to-digest artefacts, structured playbooks, talking avatars and content in different languages and formats.

The work was an exploration of the intended experience and its possible workflow. The availability of a concept does not itself establish implementation, adoption or a measured improvement in time to understanding.

04 / The learning

Good automation should preserve meaning.

I took away a stronger appreciation of the work that happens between a specialist insight and the person who must use it. The design problem is not only speed or presentation; it is preserving context and meaning across that handoff.

If I take this work further, the most important evidence would come from approved artefacts and evaluation with both expert reviewers and the intended frontline audience.

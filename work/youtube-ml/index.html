01 / Context and design challenge

What needed to change?

Machine-learning operations often rely on people who must process large amounts of information consistently. Review interfaces can increase cognitive load when evidence, instructions and decision controls compete for attention.

02 / Approach and contribution

Making complexity legible.

The design focus was information hierarchy: what reviewers need first, which signals should be compared and how the interface can support repeatable decisions. The work explored workflow clarity, interaction patterns and operational efficiency.

03 / Design rationale

Why these decisions matter.

Rather than treating every field as equally important, the experience approach organises tasks around the decision being made. Clear status, contextual detail and predictable controls help users remain oriented throughout repeated review cycles.

04 / Outcomes, limitations and reflection

Impact with context.

Professional materials report a throughput improvement of approximately 40%; the public portfolio does not establish a controlled causal attribution.

01 / The brief beneath the brief

What I was really trying to solve.

The original request sounded like an analytics problem. As I got closer to the workflow, the challenge was more human: people working with machine-learning models needed to find the right classifier information and understand what it meant without digging through a complicated dashboard.

02 / My contribution

What I worked through.

I researched the existing experience, facilitated design-thinking sessions and developed scalable prototypes. I paid particular attention to discoverability, the way analysis was organised and the relationship between information and the decisions people needed to make.

03 / Reflection

What stayed with me.

I learned to be wary of treating a complex dashboard as a visual-design problem. The most useful work happens when you understand which decisions the dashboard is supposed to support.

The 2023 project record describes research, workshops and prototypes; it does not establish a measured production impact.

UX lens / Design reasoning

The question behind the interface.

How can reviewers make consistent decisions when the information environment is dense and repetitive?

01 · Human need

Understand the user

Cognitive load, task focus and error prevention. The interface should reflect how people interpret information, recognise risk and decide what to do next.

02 · Experience strategy

Structure the interaction

Prioritised signals, consistent review patterns, clear status and escalation. This is an interpretation of the design approach rather than a claim that every technique was used in a documented research session.

03 · Design principle

Make the outcome legible

Less ambiguity between inspection and action. The experience should help people understand the state of the system and retain meaningful control.

01 / The brief

A dashboard can hide an operational problem.

YouTube had an Xray platform for understanding content performance and machine-learning processes. Different teams managed different models, releases, updates and documentation in ways that were inconsistent and scattered.

The request initially appeared to be about data analytics and the way information was displayed. It was easy to see why the dashboard mattered. It took more investigation to see the experience surrounding it.

02 / What I noticed

The people managing the models needed a shared picture.

The difficulty wasn't only interpreting numbers. Teams needed to understand which models they were working with, what had changed, how performance could be monitored and where to find related knowledge.

That shifted the design question. Instead of asking only what information Xray should display, I wanted to explore how its operational experience could help people make sense of work happening across teams.

The most valuable change was in how we framed the problem—not in how many charts could fit on a screen.
03 / My contribution

Laying groundwork for an operational layer.

I investigated the existing experience and helped frame a design direction for clearer monitoring, visibility and coordination. The proposed operational layer was intended to make the machine-learning ecosystem easier for teams to build upon.

We also considered whether this direction might inform other Google-related work. It remained an ambition at the design-groundwork stage; I cannot present it as a deployed capability or a proven multi-team rollout.

04 / What stayed with me

The first request is not always the real design problem.

Enterprise interfaces often reveal the visible symptom of a much larger workflow challenge. This project taught me to look beyond the dashboard and understand the decisions, relationships and responsibilities around it.

A coherent operational model can be more valuable than polishing a fragmented interface. Whether the proposed solution achieved that would require subsequent implementation and evaluation evidence.

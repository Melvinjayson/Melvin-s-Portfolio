01 / The brief beneath the brief

What I was really trying to solve.

Immigration processes can carry real consequences for the people navigating them. The brief was to explore a self-service experience where people could understand requirements, manage appointments and see what was happening with their application.

02 / My contribution

What I worked through.

As part of an Accenture RFP response, I helped design the MyImmigration self-service portal concept, including appointment booking, application tracking and an AI-assisted help channel. The challenge was to make a complex service legible without oversimplifying it.

What stayed with me.

I took away a sharper appreciation for designing around uncertainty: when someone is waiting for an important decision, visibility and understandable language are not decorative features.

UX lens / Design reasoning

The question behind the interface.

How can complex public requirements be made understandable without losing accuracy?

01 · Human need

Understand the user

Inclusive service design, plain language and information architecture. The interface should reflect how people interpret information, recognise risk and decide what to do next.

02 · Experience strategy

Structure the interaction

Stepwise journeys, contextual instructions and transparent progress. This is an interpretation of the design approach rather than a claim that every technique was used in a documented research session.

03 · Design principle

Make the outcome legible

Reduced avoidable confusion in consequential interactions. The experience should help people understand the state of the system and retain meaningful control.

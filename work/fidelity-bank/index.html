01 / The situation

The mobile app was only part of the problem.

Fidelity Bank was facing customer attrition in a market where digital banking had become central to customers' expectations. The mobile experience needed attention, and the bank had already invested substantially in its digital ecosystem.

What became clear was that investment in customer-facing platforms did not automatically create a coherent experience inside the organisation.

02 / What I found

Customers and employees were facing connected friction.

Customers needed easier ways to transact and communicate problems. The contact-centre experience was weak, and feedback and updates were difficult to surface coherently.

Employees were also working across fragmented tools and processes. Those gaps made it harder to coordinate decisions and manage delivery end-to-end. Improving one side of the experience without understanding the other would leave important constraints untouched.

My job was to help the customer's needs make sense to the people building and operating the bank's services.
03 / What I led

Connecting research, design, engineering and leadership.

I led the design and engineering team. Through design research and empathy mapping, we worked to understand customer needs and designed a new digital banking experience and ecosystem, including internal operational tools.

I acted as a UX bridge across teams and levels of responsibility. My contribution was not simply a set of screens; it was helping connect what customers needed with the systems, decisions and people required to deliver a better experience.

04 / Results and reflection

The wider outcome was encouraging; attribution still matters.

I reported an almost 40% increase in customer satisfaction and higher transaction volumes as project outcomes. Those figures should not be treated as proof that UX design alone caused the changes, and I do not attribute market-share recovery or an industry ranking to this work.

The most enduring lesson was that an organisation can invest in digital transformation and still leave people disconnected. The experience belongs to the whole service—not only the mobile interface.
